Thessaros: the Guide

Spoilers. This guide contains every solution, the language, and the ending. It is for whoever runs, tests or extends the game.

Running it

Open index.html in any current desktop or mobile browser. There is no build step and no server; the only network request is for two Google fonts, and the game falls back to Georgia without them. Progress is saved in that browser under the key thessaros.v3. To jump ahead while testing, open index.html?dev: a panel offers Know the script, All five keys (puts you before the Door with five unturned keys), Door opened and Reset.

The previous version stored a campaign under thessaros2. Its puzzles no longer exist, so the new game never reads it: a player with an old save sees a one-line notice on the title screen and begins a fresh campaign. The old data is left untouched.

The story underneath

None of this is told outright. It is the history the inscriptions were written to agree with.

  • The deep. Before the city, the plain had a chasm. Its people, later called the Feeders, carried their dead to the edge and gave them to it. They called it nhaur, the deep, and believed death was a destination, not an ending: everything falls towards it.
  • Ishvel. A woman, Ishvel, went down into the chasm and came back. She was the first to write the Tongue. Her testament says the deep is neither stone nor nothing; an eye saw her; the deep has a name, Nhaurith, she who is the deep. The abyss is not where she lives. It is her.
  • The opening. Generations later her Eye began to open. Whatever its gaze fell on ended: gods, names, rivers. The Keepers took power from the Feeders and built Thessaros as a lid. Seen from above the temple is a closed eye: the Door at the End is its pupil, the city its eyelid, and the five chambers sit along the lid like stitches.
  • The five keys are five scales of her own body (ghaz means both key and scale), each holding one seal of the lid. They were hidden inside mechanisms that only a reader of the Tongue could operate, because the Keepers believed only a reader would understand what not to do.
  • The end of the city. The Keepers came to believe the dead were not destroyed below but sleeping, safe only while she could not see them. So they masked the dead, sealed their eyes, broke their names, and lowered them. At the last, the whole city did this to itself. Thessaros was not abandoned. It went down.
  • The reader. Ishvel's last line: Nhaurith sees the one who reads. Every word the player learns brings them closer to seeing her, and her to seeing them. The first words over the Threshold, unreadable at the start, were Akh nhaur ven. Ka val.: the eye of the deep is watching; do not go down.
  • Orvane, an earlier explorer, left notes at the Threshold, in the Stacks and on the Keepers' Stair. Orvane learned to read, understood, turned back, and vanished.

The map (M) lets the player travel back to any place already visited. Unexplored passages appear as “?”. After the player sees the ceiling plan in Chamber V, the map draws itself as a closed eye.

The temple responds. Black growths with turquoise points spread as keys are taken. After the third key, the hooded statue in the Upper Ring has turned to face the shaft. Lamps burn without oil. The cistern drains when the Engine is set. After Chamber V, new letters have appeared on Ishvel's wall.

The Tongue

The script

Each letter is a small serpent with a head and 0–4 barbs across its body. The head names the row; the barbs count along it; the rows are the player's own alphabet in order.

Head01234
Eye (the vowels)AEIOU
Hook, leftBCDFG
Hook, rightHJKLM
FangsNPQRS
CrownTVWXY
BarZ

A small diamond ends a sentence. Every vowel wears an eye.

Grammar

  • The deed comes last. Doer, then what is acted on, then the verb: Nhaur zarguk sha, the deep devours every god. A place a thing goes to stands before the verb: Valaruk nhaur val, the dead go down into the deep.
  • The thing, then its owner. Akh nhaur, the eye of the deep. Durn krul, the door of the end.
  • No word for “is”. Nhaur krul: the deep is the end.
  • KA, not, stands before the deed it refuses. On its own it commands: Ka val, do not go down. With no deed it ends the clause: Nhaur vorz ka, the deep is not stone.
  • Suffixes: -UK all, many; -AR one who does it (or works with it); -OS place of; -ITH she who is. They stack: thess·ar·uk, keep-one-who-all, the keepers; thess·ar·os, the place of the keepers.

Derived words in the temple: thessar keeper, thessaruk the keepers, Thessaros place of the keepers, valaruk the dead, valos place of going down, zhaalar reader, zhaalos place of words, zhaalith she who speaks (Ishvel's title), krulith she who is the end (the Feeders' name for her), Nhaurith she who is the deep, shemos place of names, murros place of sleep, akhos place of the eye.

Her true name, Nhaurith, is written in full exactly once, in Ishvel's testament. Every other inscription truncates it to nhaur, which the player reads as “the deep”, a place. The Feeders call her Krulith. Her title in the player's language, the One Below, is how a reader would render nhaur once they know it is a person.

Solutions

The first reading (Threshold) THESSAROS

The surveyor's marker gives the name; Orvane's note points out that signs 4, 5 and 9 repeat. Writing a sound under a sign fills every copy of it. Teaches T H E S A R O.

The scribes' wall (Hall of Letters)

The wall is the table above. The player fills unknown signs by selecting one and choosing a letter. Wrong letters simply refuse. Completing any two rows (for example I and U on the Eye row, plus N P Q on the Fang row) shows the system and fills in the rest. The inner door's slab then sinks.

Hints: the top row all wear the Eye, and A, E, O are among them; the last row has a single sign, like the last letter of an alphabet; the rows run in alphabetical order.

I. The Hall of Names

Turn each prism to the name of the thing beneath it, then pull the lever. The lever reports how many are right without saying which.

stone VORZ · lamp LUM · key GHAZ · door DURN · serpent OUROTH · eye AKH

Each word is carved elsewhere beside the thing it names: lum by every lamp niche, vorz on unused blocks, ouroth on the serpent statue, ghaz on the scribe's key tag, durn over doors. Akh follows by elimination from the mural (ouroth akh thess under a serpent and an eye). Optional: lift the seventh prism (nhaur) to find shem nhaur ka zha.

II. The Hall of Words

Find six fragments: three lying on the floor; one under the rubble (drag the blocks aside); one on the high ledge (climb the toppled statue); one in a sealed jar in the Stacks, through the crack in the right wall. A seventh, warmer piece also lies on the floor. It is from the Feeders' stele and fits the middle-left place just as well.

Each piece only seats where its border and breaks match. With the forged piece in the middle-left, the stele stays dead: the words across the middle seam do not form words (…kru|ur ghul). The right middle-left piece is the one from the rubble. Reward: eight words and four rules.

III. The Engine of Sleep

Three rings with 8, 6 and 4 marks start at positions 3, 1 and 2. The goal is all three serpent heads at the closed eye (position 0). The right crank's handle lies in the Cistern water.

  • Left crank: outer +1, middle −1.
  • Right crank: middle +1, inner +1.

Cranks only turn forwards. The insight is that a full revolution of one ring returns it to the same place but drags the ring it is geared to. Shortest solution: left ×5, right ×10 (also left ×13 and right ×6, or left ×21 and right ×2). Solving it drains the cistern and uncovers the mosaic.

IV. The Chapel of the Last Rite

Read the litany on the bier, verb last in every sentence. Each station performs one deed.

  1. Table: break the name-tablet (shem krak)
  2. Basin: pour water over the body (sketh orr)
  3. Wax: seal the eyes (akhuk thess)
  4. Mask: lay it over the face (mul dal)
  5. Bell: ring it (khol zha)
  6. Winch: lower the bier with your eyes turned away (sketh nhaur val + the relief thessar ka ven)

Out of order, the rite resets. Covering open eyes with the mask is refused outright. Watching the bier go down shows two turquoise eyes far below and resets the rite. On success the bier comes back up empty except for the key, and reveals the line beneath it.

V. The Cell of Ishvel

A 7×7 floor of lenses (each bearing a glyph) and eight turnable mirrors over a crack of turquoise light. The light spells whatever it passes through. The Keepers' setting (mirrors 1–4 as / / \ /) spells nhaur into the black stone: the containment. The goal is to spell her whole name, written once in the testament, and end on the reader's seat.

N → H → A → U → R, then up through I, right through T, up, left through H onto the SEAT

From the starting state, turn mirrors 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7 and 8 once each, leaving mirror 6 (left edge). A near miss spells nhaurith and runs on through S into rock. Reading the testament teaches -ith and reveals that nhaur was her name all along; the ceiling plan teaches that durn is also the lid of an eye.

The Door at the End

  1. Locks, in any order. I: turn the prism to shem (the word on the bone key), then insert. II: slide both stone halves aside, insert. III: insert, then six half-turns (three full turns, one per spiral groove). IV: insert, then hold the lever down for about 2.5 seconds; releasing early lets it spring back. The “Press once instead of holding” setting removes the need to hold. V: insert, then turn the glass key until it lies along the slanting crack of light (one turn).
  2. Plates lie one over another and lift off top first: ZHAAL, VAL, SHEM, AKH, MURR (each is marked by its word). A pinned plate names the one on top of it.
  3. Wheel: set the word at the notch to GHUL (open) and throw the lever. Every other word has its own failure: thess seals it tighter, ven shows a line of light, murr, sha and val each do something unsettling.
  4. Activation: the locks answer in turn, the rock shakes, the seam shines, and the lid lifts. The first viewing cannot be skipped. Afterwards the door can be replayed with a Skip button.

Below: the reveal

About 38 seconds before any words: darkness (0–4.5 s); faint shapes and turquoise points (4.5–10); two eyes, her face faintly lit (10–16.5); the eyes go out and the screen is fully dark (16.5–19.5); her head and coils emerge (19.5–27); the coils wind into a spiral as the head is drawn aside (27–34); the Eye opens (31–36), a riser builds, and at 36.4 the pupil snaps to a slit as she lunges at the viewer (flash, zoom, shake, a screaming stinger). From 37.5 dozens of small eyes open along her coils. Then four lines, one at a time, glossed only as far as the player can read them, and her name. After the first viewing a Skip button appears; Settings offers to watch it again.

The door needs no last click: once it has opened, the player is pulled down into the reveal. The Eye is live. It follows the pointer (the coil eyes each look at it separately), blinks on its own, and wanders when the pointer is still or leaves the window. Its pupil narrows as the pointer comes near or moves fast. Her heartbeat starts in the darkness at 16.5 s and speeds up the closer the pointer gets. Clicking or tapping makes her flinch and hiss; three quick clicks and she lunges again. Easter egg: tap inside the great Eye ten times (the count resets after 6 seconds without a tap; her veins redden with each one) and she eats you (about 10 s). The Eye slams shut and the dark goes silent. Her eyes ignite one by one, then her face appears, seen head-on: scaled hide, ragged mane, angry brows. Her forked tongue flicks twice. She rears back on her coiled neck, rattling, jaws gaping to show fangs and rings of teeth down her throat. Then she strikes. The view goes inside her mouth as the jaws close, there is a red flash, she chews once, and you slide down her throat. Then: “Nhaurith does not like to be touched. She lets you go. This time.” The scene comes back, and the journal gains the lore entry Inside. The score (sub drone, growl, a choir of formants, trembling strings, an endlessly falling tone, whispers) swells with that tension. Reduced motion turns off blinking, shake and the lunge zoom.

Hints

The hint button (H) gives three tiers for whatever the player most needs right now. It knows the current room: inside a chamber it helps with that chamber, and outside one it points to the next step. The last tier states the answer.
